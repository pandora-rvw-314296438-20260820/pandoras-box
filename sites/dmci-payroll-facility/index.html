Capital architecture

Keep the funder, lender, employer and technology roles clean.

The model separates capital custody from digital orchestration. The TSP can run the experience and the lender can originate the credit without either party needing to sit on the entire committed facility.

Capital is committed before it is consumed.

If the agreed facility were ₱30 million and a qualifying employee were approved for ₱30,000, only that loan-level amount would need to be deployed for the transaction. The remaining committed capacity stays available for future eligible loans.

Risk and control

Payroll deduction improves collection. It does not erase credit risk.

Under the contemplated payroll-deduction-only structure, employee credit risk remains with the lending/funding structure unless additional protection is separately negotiated.

↗

Employment separation

Resignation, termination or extended unpaid leave can interrupt salary deductions. The lender needs a direct repayment and collections process after separation.

₱

Payroll sufficiency

Salary may become insufficient for the scheduled deduction. Rules should define affordability, prioritization, exception handling and borrower notification.

✓

Fraud and eligibility

Identity, employment, account and application fraud require verification controls, audit trails and review paths for exceptions.

◎

Employer concentration

Even with many borrowers, the portfolio is concentrated around one employer and should be assessed accordingly by the capital provider.

↻

Operational resilience

Payroll files, bank settlement, duplicate instructions, reconciliation and system downtime require idempotency, exception queues and traceable recovery.

§

Regulatory structure

Lending, disclosures, data privacy, electronic transactions and wage-deduction requirements must be validated in the final Philippine legal and compliance design.

Funding proposition

Not a ₱30M transfer.
A controlled lending infrastructure.

The institutional opportunity is to provide capital against a defined employee-lending program where each qualifying loan is verified, documented, funded and tracked individually—and where collections can flow back through an authorized payroll mechanism.
